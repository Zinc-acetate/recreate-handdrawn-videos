# Production Workflow and Handoffs

Advance through evidence-based checkpoints, not counts of generated files. Use the timing, visual, and delivery references for technical details; this checklist defines working outputs and advancement conditions.

## Stages and Exit Conditions

1. **Lock inputs and constraints.** Verify source video, identity references, replacement scope, fidelity/adaptation policy, target format, and delivery method. Record versions, checksums, actual timebase, and audio provenance. Check available tools. Produce a constraint sheet; do not invent missing design details or silently change the production route.
2. **Build a full-video ledger.** For each shot, record source intervals, distinct drawings and exposures, characters/components, camera, text, effects, depth order, first/last visibility, and protected regions. Give each component a motion class, source evidence, and method. Map source drawings to planned assets and output intervals. An incomplete ledger cannot support a full-video completion claim.
3. **Prove the risky short section.** Choose the smallest meaningful cycle or shot containing the main difficulty. Produce identity/style anchors, corresponding key drawings, and a composited sample. Check fixed canvases, registration, alpha, contact, occlusion, and loop boundaries; play it at original speed. Fix the responsible layer before scaling up. If the user asked to see a sample first, present it and obtain approval before expanding.
4. **Expand using the ledger.** Reuse accepted assets and configurations. Complete cleanup, replacement, independent text/effect timing, and layering shot by shot. Record changed drawings, exposures, and approved approximations. Freeze locally accepted versions, then reintegrate and review boundaries and complete visibility intervals.
5. **Complete visual and technical review.** At original speed, review full cycles, neighboring shots, and the complete video. Separately decode the entire export and check timebase, audio, dimensions, color, and coverage. Record evidence and unchecked scope; still inspection is not playback review.
6. **Freeze and deliver.** Retain target-resolution assets, master/viewing copies, relative-path projects, exact dependencies, a rerun entry point, mappings, and review results. Verify asset loading and rerun representative difficult frames independently; list anything not reproduced. Deliver only through authorized routes and verify accessibility. Exported does not mean uploaded or published.

## Record at Each Handoff or Stage Boundary

- Goal, active restrictions, approved approximations, and pending decisions.
- Current stage, last accepted version, next executable step, and blockers.
- Project-relative input/adopted-asset paths, versions/checksums, and separately identified identity and style anchors.
- Shot/component ledger, drawing mapping, source/output exposures, camera transforms, and layer relationships.
- Completed, unmade, repair-needed, playback-pending, and whole-video-reviewed intervals, rather than one unsupported percentage.
- Change scope, potentially affected neighboring sections, actual commands/dependencies, and failed attempts with causes.
- Evidence locations and exact review methods, delivery status, and outstanding commitments.

Keep project facts in the project handoff, not the reusable skill. Do not add account details, private paths, private asset identifiers, or unpublished project material to the generic instructions.

## Bounded Progress Updates

At the start, explain that updates will accompany viewable samples, passed stages, material plan changes, and blockers; let the user adjust frequency. Agree on a maximum quiet interval for long work, such as 5–10 minutes, unless an existing preference applies. If that interval passes without a new result, briefly report actual progress, the current issue, and the next step. Avoid speculative percentages and repeated empty reassurance. This does not authorize scheduled tasks, third-party messages, or other external actions.
